# Plain English

Read alongside the relevant medium reference when the task calls for plain English, explanations outside the reader's field, non-native readability, or translation preparation. Use the reader's actual knowledge; an expert who uses English as an additional language may still know the domain well.

## Make the explanation accessible

Organize the passage around what the reader needs to find, understand, and do. Bring an unfamiliar concept into the explanation before relying on it. Use a familiar, accurate equivalent or explain the necessary technical term in context using supplied facts. A definition needs enough detail to help this reader; it has no fixed word limit. Do not add a diagnosis, example, or operational detail merely to make the text sound concrete.

Unpack a dense noun chain when the relationship between its parts is unclear. Make pronoun references explicit where readers could reasonably choose different referents. Prefer a literal expression to an unfamiliar idiom when that improves comprehension or translation. Keep complete grammar and connective words so that shorter sentences still form a coherent explanation. Preserve the document's English spelling convention.

Use consistent terms for the same concept while keeping different concepts distinct. For example, validating a payload and verifying a signature can be different operations; settings and command-line options can be different parts of a product. A vocabulary substitution must preserve the operation, object, and meaning.

For action sequences, use the procedure guidance in [Technical prose](technical.md). For translation preparation, also keep terminology, references, and conditions explicit across the passage. Preparing a source for translation does not authorize translating it.

## Boundaries of controlled English

ASD-STE100 is a controlled language with writing rules and a controlled dictionary, developed for technical documentation. Its discipline can inform plain English, but this branch does not implement or audit the full standard. A request for STE compliance needs the applicable official issue, its vocabulary rules, and review against the actual requirements. Identify any unverified compliance rather than treating a plain-language edit as certification.

Do not import blanket bans on modal verbs, perfect tenses, passive voice, contractions, or punctuation. Preserve recommendations, permission, uncertainty, completion, and ongoing relevance. In particular, a recommendation expressed by `should` must not become an obligation expressed by `must`. Prefer active voice when the actor is known and useful; do not invent an actor to avoid a passive construction. Fixed sentence lengths and automatic word swaps are not default quality criteria.
